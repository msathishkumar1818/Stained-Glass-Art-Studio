42 Years of Provenance
Our Living History

Crafting in the Shadows of Edinburgh Castle

In the autumn of 1984, master glazier Alistair MacIntyre returned to Scotland after seven years of rigorous apprenticeship across the cathedral workshops of York Minster and Chartres. He established Stained Glass Art Studio in a former cooperage along the Royal Mile, equipped only with a wooden glazing easel, an antique diamond scorer, and a chest of mouthblown German cylinder glass.

Over four decades, our team has expanded to four master artisans and conservators. While architectural styles have evolved from Victorian restorations to minimalist contemporary skylights, our fundamental tenets remain unchanged: every came is hand-stretched, every joint is hand-soldered, and every panel is cemented to withstand centuries of Scottish gales.

Our Guiding Tenets

The Four Pillars of Our Craft

Principles forged over forty years at the glazing bench that guide every single piece leaving our studio.

01

Light as Living Medium

Unlike paint, stained glass does not reflect daylight; it transmutes it. We compose each work by analyzing the sun's seasonal path across your specific room.

02

Ancient European Lineage

We source mouthblown cylinder glass from historic glassmakers like Glashütte Lamberts in Bavaria, preserving natural bubbles, variations, and luminous refraction.

03

Permanent Came Integrity

No shortcuts with adhesive overlays. We work purely in structural lead came, hand-cemented with linseed oil and lampblack for a weatherproof bond built for generations.

04

Eco & Heritage Reclaim

Every remnant off-cut is salvaged and recycled. We use organic, non-toxic plant-based fluxing oils and maintain zero hazardous wastewater runoff in our Old Town facility.

Four Decades

Studio Heritage Milestones

Key historical moments in our journey from a solitary workbench to Scotland's premier architectural glass atelier.

1984

Studio Founded on the Royal Mile

Alistair MacIntyre opens the atelier doors in Edinburgh's historic Old Town, focusing on authentic Victorian tenement vestibule restoration.

1996

St Giles Cathedral Annex Restoration

Awarded the comprehensive conservation contract to stabilize and restore 14 damaged 19th-century Gothic lancet windows.

2008

British Society Fellowship Induction

Inducted as an Accredited Fellow of the British Society of Master Glass Painters for contributions to heritage craftsmanship education.

2018

Contemporary Architectural Glass Launch

Partnering with award-winning Scottish architects to introduce minimalist geometric light-well panels into modern sustainable residences.

2026

The Glass Academy Expansion

Opening our upgraded public teaching benches to educate the next generation of UK glass artists, hobbyists, and preservationists.
